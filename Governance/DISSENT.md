# Recording and Revisiting Dissent

## Purpose

This policy preserves minority positions after a governance decision so future
maintainers can understand the alternatives considered and revisit decisions
when circumstances materially change.

## Recording dissent

A participant who disagrees with a proposed decision may record their dissent
in the decision's issue, pull request, or meeting minutes before the decision
is finalized. The record should include:

- the decision and the recorded outcome;
- the dissenting position and its rationale;
- any evidence, risks, or alternatives offered; and
- any condition or date that the dissenter believes should trigger review.

The decision record links to the original discussion and preserves dissent as
part of the permanent history. A dissent does not prevent an approved decision
from being implemented.

Dissent should be attributed to its author unless they request otherwise. For a
security, Code of Conduct, or personnel matter, the record contains only a
non-sensitive summary and follows the applicable confidentiality process.

## Revisiting a decision

A decision may be reconsidered when:

- material new evidence, technical information, or a changed external
  requirement is available;
- the implemented outcome materially differs from the approved decision or
  causes an unanticipated impact;
- a conflict of interest or procedural error affected the original decision; or
- a review condition recorded with the original decision has been met.

A request to revisit a decision is made in the original decision record or a
new issue that links to it. The request states the decision to reconsider, the
new information or changed condition, the proposed outcome, and whether urgent
action is required.

The applicable voters decide whether to reopen the matter and, if reopened,
make the new decision using the same quorum and approval requirement as the
original decision. The reopened record retains the original outcome and dissent
alongside the new decision. Repeating a vote without new grounds is not a
reason to revisit a settled decision.
