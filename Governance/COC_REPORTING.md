# Code of Conduct Incident Reporting Process

This document describes how community members report Code of Conduct (CoC)
violations, how those reports are handled, and how confidentiality is
protected throughout the process.

## Who handles reports

Reports are received and investigated by the **CoC Committee**, which consists
of at least two maintainers who are not named in the report. The current
committee members and their contact details are listed in
[MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md) under the "CoC Committee" section.

If every committee member is named in a report, the TSC will designate an
independent reviewer from outside the project.

## How to submit a report

Reports may be submitted through any of the following channels:

1. **Email** – send a message to the private address listed in
   [MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md) under "CoC Committee". This address is
   monitored only by committee members.
2. **Direct message** – contact any committee member privately via the
   project's communication platform (see [COMMUNICATION.md](COMMUNICATION.md)).
3. **Anonymous form** – if the project provides an anonymous reporting tool,
   its link is listed in [MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md). Anonymous reports
   limit the committee's ability to follow up but will still be investigated.

Do not file reports in public issue trackers, pull requests, or chat channels.

## What to include in a report

A report should include as much of the following as possible:

- Your contact information (unless submitting anonymously).
- Names or identifiers of the individuals involved.
- Description of the behaviour and why it may violate the Code of Conduct.
- Links, screenshots, or other evidence (attach files to the email or message).
- Date, time, and platform where the behaviour occurred.
- Names of any witnesses.
- Whether the incident is ongoing.

Incomplete reports are still accepted; the committee will follow up for
additional detail when needed.

## Process after submission

| Step | Owner | Target timeline |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| Acknowledge receipt | Committee | Within **48 hours** |
| Initial triage and committee assignment | Committee | Within **5 business days** |
| Investigation (interviews, evidence review) | Committee | Within **14 days** of acknowledgement |
| Decision and response | Committee | Within **21 days** of acknowledgement |
| Appeal window opens | Reporter / Respondent | 7 days after decision |

Timelines may be extended for complex cases; the committee will notify all
parties of any delay.

## Confidentiality

- The identity of the reporter is kept confidential from the respondent and
  from the public unless the reporter explicitly consents to disclosure or
  disclosure is required by law.
- Details of the report and investigation are shared only with committee
  members and, where necessary, with legal counsel or platform administrators.
- The committee will not disclose investigation details in public channels. A
  brief, anonymised outcome summary may be shared with the community when
  doing so serves a clear educational or safety purpose, and only after all
  parties are informed.
- Committee members recuse themselves from any case in which they have a
  conflict of interest.

## Possible outcomes

After investigation the committee may take any proportionate action, including
but not limited to:

- No action (report not substantiated).
- A private written warning to the respondent.
- A request for a public or private apology.
- Temporary suspension from project spaces.
- Permanent removal from project spaces.
- Escalation to the platforms or employers involved.

The reporter and respondent are both informed of the outcome in writing.

## Appeals

Either party may appeal a decision within seven days of receiving the outcome
by notifying the CoC Committee in writing with the grounds for appeal. Appeals
are reviewed by committee members who did not make the original decision, or
by an independent reviewer if no such member is available. The appeal decision
is final.

## No retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who reports a CoC incident in good faith, assists
in an investigation, or appeals a decision is itself a CoC violation and will
be treated accordingly.
